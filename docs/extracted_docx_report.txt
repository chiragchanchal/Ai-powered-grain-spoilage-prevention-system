ABSTRACT
Post-harvest grain spoilage remains a severe structural threat to agricultural livelihoods and regional food security, particularly among rural storage godowns, Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), and village agricultural cooperatives that rely on periodic manual inspections. Unchecked increases in grain moisture content, internal ambient temperature, and interstitial relative humidity—frequently triggered by sudden unseasonal weather shifts or monsoon transitions—rapidly accelerate fungal proliferation, insect infestation, and mycotoxin contamination well before visible clumping or musty odors appear.
To address this real-world community challenge, this project presents the AI-Powered Grain Spoilage Prevention System for Storage Facilities, an early-warning web analytics and decision-support platform designed to forecast spoilage risk and prescribe timely storage interventions. The architecture integrates high-throughput multi-parameter environmental telemetry (internal temperature, relative humidity, and grain moisture content) persisted in Apache Cassandra, live and 48–72 hour meteorological forecasts retrieved from the OpenWeatherMap API, and historical climate-to-spoilage correlations modeled inside a Neo4j Knowledge Graph. A Python orchestration backend synthesizes these time-series, meteorological, and graph-derived features into a dual machine learning risk assessment engine built with TensorFlow 2.x and ensemble tree classifiers (scikit-learn RandomForestClassifier), outputting calibrated Low, Medium, and High spoilage risk alerts alongside non-technical preventive aeration and drying guidelines via an interactive web dashboard.
Validated through structured community stakeholder walkthroughs and simulated/field storage scenarios, the system demonstrated proactive risk detection ahead of critical moisture and fungal thresholds ([To be filled: observed high-risk recall and accuracy after final benchmark]). By enabling low-cost, preventive intervention against avoidable post-harvest grain losses, the project directly advances UN SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) and UN SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), while supporting climate-adaptive resilience under UN SDG 13 (Climate Action).
KEYWORDS: Community Service, Post-Harvest Grain Storage, Machine Learning, TensorFlow, Random Forest, Neo4j Knowledge Graph, Apache Cassandra, OpenWeatherMap API, UN SDG 2, UN SDG 12.
Chapter 1: Introduction & Background
1.1 Background of the Problem
Agriculture forms the economic backbone of rural livelihoods across India, especially across northern grain-producing belts including Haryana, Punjab, and western Uttar Pradesh. While decades of agronomic investment have expanded India's food grain production, a substantial share of harvested wheat, paddy, maize, and pulses is still lost after harvest during handling, transit, and storage. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) Food Loss and Food Waste Policy Portal and The State of Food and Agriculture (SOFA) 2019, roughly 14% of food produced globally—valued at an estimated USD 400 billion annually (13.2% under updated FAO Food Loss Index tracking)—is lost between harvest and the retail stage.
Within India, national surveys commissioned by the Ministry of Food Processing Industries and reported in Lok Sabha via Press Information Bureau (PIB) Delhi Release ID 1885038 quantify harvest and post-harvest losses across major agricultural commodities in two benchmark studies: the Indian Council of Agricultural Research – Central Institute of Post-Harvest Engineering and Technology (ICAR-CIPHET, 2015) study and the NABARD Consultancy Services (NABCONS, 2022) study. For cereals alone, national harvest and post-harvest losses ranged from 4.65% to 5.99% (ICAR-CIPHET, 2015) and remained substantial at 3.89% to 5.92% (NABCONS, 2022), while pulses experienced 5.65% to 6.74% loss in 2022.

Biologically, stored grain is a living, respiring commodity. As documented in the FAO Manual of the Prevention of Post-Harvest Grain Losses (Section 2.2.5) and FAO Crop Handling, Conditioning and Storage Guidelines, safe long-term storage depends on maintaining equilibrium relative humidity at or below 65% and grain moisture content within safe thresholds (typically 12%–14% depending on crop and storage duration). When interstitial relative humidity crosses 65%–70%, mold and fungal colonies begin proliferating and emitting toxic secondary metabolites (mycotoxins). Simultaneously, stored-product insects achieve peak reproductive rates at temperatures between 28°C and 33°C and relative humidities of 60%–80% (FAO Manual, Section 2.2.5). Respiration from fungi and pests generates metabolic heat and additional moisture, triggering self-reinforcing localized "hot spots" inside silos and bag stacks that cause caking, discoloration, foul odors, and economic rejection at local mandis.

1.2 Community / Stakeholder Context
Our community service engagement focuses on local agricultural grain storage stakeholders—including rural godown operators, storage cooperative managers, and smallholder farming groups [To be filled: Community Partner Name, Organization Type, and Location]. During Month-1 requirement-gathering interactions, stakeholders identified three recurring operational pain points:
Blindness to Internal Microclimate Drift: Operators check stacks periodically by hand or smell, lacking visibility into internal core heating or rising moisture inside deep grain piles until spoilage is already widespread.
Vulnerability to Unseasonal Weather & Monsoon Spikes: Sudden rises in ambient humidity or pre-monsoon rainfall cause rapid moisture reabsorption in semi-ventilated godowns, yet operators have no integrated tool correlating incoming 48–72 hour weather forecasts with current bin conditions.
Lack of Actionable, Non-Technical Decision Cues: Raw sensor numbers or complex meteorological charts are difficult for rural warehouse staff to translate into immediate operational steps (such as when to run aeration fans versus keeping vents sealed during high-humidity outside air).
1.3 Why a Technical Solution
Manual inspection is fundamentally reactive: once musty odors, caking, or visible surface mold appear, a significant fraction of the grain stock has already lost marketable grade or nutritional safety. At the opposite extreme, industrial silo automation platforms from commercial vendors require high capital outlays for proprietary cables, cellular gateways, and annual cloud subscriptions that are financially out of reach for rural Indian cooperatives. A software-first, AI-driven early warning platform bridges this gap by combining low-cost telemetry streams, open-tier meteorological APIs, graph-based historical climate context, and predictive machine learning classifiers inside an intuitive web dashboard—shifting community storage management from reactive loss reporting to proactive spoilage prevention.
Chapter 2: Problem Statement
Rural grain storage facilities, local godowns, and farming cooperatives experience preventable post-harvest grain loss and quality degradation because they lack continuous microclimate visibility and predictive spoilage intelligence. Existing manual monitoring practices fail to correlate real-time internal storage parameters (temperature, relative humidity, and grain moisture content) with incoming meteorological anomalies and historical regional climate risks. Therefore, there is an urgent need to engineer an AI-powered early warning system for post-harvest grain spoilage in storage facilities that integrates environmental telemetry, live weather forecasts, and historical climate knowledge graphs to accurately forecast spoilage risk in advance and guide storage operators with clear, timely preventive interventions for maximum yield preservation.

Chapter 4: Objectives
The technical solution is engineered to achieve five clear, measurable objectives:
Continuous Multi-Parameter Microclimate Ingestion: Capture, validate, and persist real-time storage telemetry (ambient temperature, relative humidity, and grain moisture content) across multiple storage units using a scalable time-series database (Apache Cassandra).
Weather-Aware Contextual Enrichment: Integrate live meteorological observations and 48–72 hour regional forecasts from the OpenWeatherMap API with historical regional climate-spoilage correlations stored in a Neo4j Knowledge Graph.
High-Recall Predictive Spoilage Risk Classification: Train and deploy a hybrid machine learning inference engine (Random Forest ensemble + TensorFlow 2.x neural classifier) to classify storage risk into Low, Medium, and High tiers while prioritizing High-Risk Recall so critical spoilage events are not missed.
Actionable Operator Decision Support: Deliver an accessible, non-technical web dashboard featuring live silo gauges, weather overlays, color-coded risk alerts, and specific preventive aeration, ventilation, and drying guidelines.
Community Validation & Ethical Field Testing: Validate usability, alert clarity, and operational relevance through structured walkthroughs and feedback sessions with local grain storage stakeholders in strict compliance with CS002 data ethics guidelines.

Chapter 6: Methodology
6.1 System Architecture / Flow Diagram
The system implements a decoupled four-tier data-to-decision architecture that cleanly separates real-time telemetry ingestion, polyglot persistence (time-series + graph), predictive AI inference, and community operator presentation.
Figure 6.1: Four-Tier System Architecture and End-to-End Data Flow of the AI-Powered Grain Spoilage Prevention Platform.
End-to-End Data Flow Explanation:
Tier 1 — Telemetry & Meteorological Ingestion: Storage units emit periodic environmental readings—internal ambient temperature (°C), interstitial relative humidity (%), and grain moisture content (%)—via IoT sensor nodes or structured telemetry streams. Concurrently, a scheduled Python worker polls the OpenWeatherMap API using facility latitude/longitude coordinates to fetch current external conditions and 48–72 hour temperature, humidity, and precipitation forecasts.
Tier 2 — Hybrid Polyglot Persistence: High-frequency time-series telemetry records are written append-only to Apache Cassandra, partitioned by (facility_id, silo_id, date_bucket) and clustered by descending event_timestamp for low-latency rolling-window queries. Simultaneously, contextual relationships linking geographic regions, seasonal climate patterns, grain varieties (wheat, paddy, maize), and historical spoilage incidents are stored and queried inside a Neo4j Knowledge Graph.
Tier 3 — Python Orchestration & Predictive AI Inference: The Python backend fuses recent windowed telemetry (computing rates of change ΔT/Δt and ΔRH/Δt, dew-point proximity, and Equilibrium Moisture Content margins), incoming weather deltas, and Neo4j historical risk priors into a normalized feature vector. This vector is scored by both a scikit-learn RandomForestClassifier (providing interpretable feature importance and robust tabular boundaries) and a TensorFlow 2.x Multi-Layer Perceptron / sequential risk classifier, outputting a calibrated spoilage probability and Low/Medium/High risk tier.
Tier 4 — Community Presentation & Preventive Action Engine: The risk tier and live parameters are pushed via REST/JSON endpoints to the interactive web dashboard, rendering live silo gauges, regional weather overlays, color-coded alerts, and plain-language preventive instructions (e.g., "Initiate nighttime forced aeration when external RH < 55%", "Inspect center stack for thermal hotspot").

Chapter 7: Implementation
7.1 Core System Modules & Algorithms
The platform is modularized into five cohesive engineering subsystems. Figure 7.1 illustrates the complete inference and alert escalation pipeline executed during each monitoring cycle.
Figure 7.1: End-to-End Machine Learning Inference, Risk Calibration, and Preventive Alert Escalation Flowchart.
Module 1: High-Throughput Time-Series Persistence (Apache Cassandra CQL Schema)
To guarantee O(1) partition lookups and linear write scalability across dozens of silos without relational join bottlenecks, telemetry is modeled query-first in Apache Cassandra using composite partition keys (facility_id, silo_id, date_bucket) and descending time clustering:
CREATE KEYSPACE IF NOT EXISTS grain_guard
WITH replication = {'class': 'SimpleStrategy', 'replication_factor': 1};
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS grain_guard.silo_telemetry (
    facility_id text,
    silo_id text,
    date_bucket text,         -- e.g., '2026-11' for bounded partition size
    event_ts timestamp,
    temp_c double,
    rel_humidity double,
    grain_moisture_pct double,
    ext_temp_c double,
    ext_humidity double,
    precip_prob double,
    risk_tier text,           -- 'LOW', 'MEDIUM', 'HIGH'
    PRIMARY KEY ((facility_id, silo_id, date_bucket), event_ts)
) WITH CLUSTERING ORDER BY (event_ts DESC);
Module 2: Climate-to-Spoilage Knowledge Graph (Neo4j Cypher Modeling)
Neo4j captures domain relationships between geographic climate zones, stored cereal varieties (Wheat, Paddy, Maize), threshold anomalies, and historically validated spoilage pathways (fungal mold, mycotoxin formation, weevil infestation). The Python backend queries a historical risk prior score via Cypher:
MATCH (r:Region {name: $region})-[:EXPERIENCES]->(c:ClimateEvent)
      -[:TRIGGERS {severity: 'HIGH'}]->(s:SpoilageType)
      <-[:SUSCEPTIBLE_TO]-(g:GrainType {name: $grain_type})
WHERE $forecast_rh >= c.min_rh AND $temp_c >= c.min_temp
RETURN s.name AS hazard,
       avg(c.historical_incidence_score) AS kg_risk_prior,
       collect(DISTINCT s.recommended_action) AS preventive_actions;
Module 3: Feature Engineering & Dual ML Risk Inference Engine (Python / scikit-learn / TensorFlow 2.x)
The orchestration layer extracts a 9-dimensional feature vector combining instantaneous microclimate state, 6-hour thermal/humidity drift rates (dT_dt, dRH_dt), 48-hour external weather forecast deltas, and the Neo4j historical risk prior. A weighted ensemble of Random Forest (high interpretability on tabular thresholds) and a TensorFlow 2.x dense neural network produces a calibrated probability score P(spoilage) tuned for High-Risk Recall:
import numpy as np
def assess_spoilage_risk(telemetry_window, weather_forecast, kg_prior, rf_model, tf_model):
    # 1. Compute dynamic rate-of-change features (early hotspot detection)
    t_now, rh_now, mc_now = telemetry_window[-1]["temp_c"], telemetry_window[-1]["rh"], telemetry_window[-1]["mc"]
    dt_dt = (t_now - telemetry_window[0]["temp_c"]) / max(len(telemetry_window), 1)
    drh_dt = (rh_now - telemetry_window[0]["rh"]) / max(len(telemetry_window), 1)
    # 2. Assemble normalized feature vector
    features = np.array([[
        t_now, rh_now, mc_now, dt_dt, drh_dt,
        weather_forecast["max_temp_48h"],
        weather_forecast["max_rh_48h"],
        weather_forecast["precip_prob_48h"],
        kg_prior
    ]], dtype=np.float32)
    # 3. Dual-model inference (Ensemble Random Forest + TensorFlow 2.x)
    p_rf = rf_model.predict_proba(features)[0][2]       # P(High Risk)
    p_tf = float(tf_model.predict(features, verbose=0)[0][2])
    p_combined = 0.55 * p_rf + 0.45 * p_tf
    # 4. High-recall tier classification & preventive playbook selection
    if p_combined >= 0.70 or mc_now > 14.5 or (rh_now >= 68.0 and t_now >= 28.0):
        return "HIGH", p_combined, "Immediate forced aeration (if external RH < 60%), inspect core stack for hotspots, and test moisture."
    elif p_combined >= 0.35 or mc_now > 13.0 or rh_now >= 62.0:
        return "MEDIUM", p_combined, "Schedule preventive ventilation during cool dry hours; seal vents if monsoon rain is forecast."
    return "LOW", p_combined, "Storage microclimate within safe FAO equilibrium limits; maintain routine 48-hour checks."
Module 4: Live Weather API Integration & Rate-Limit Caching
To operate reliably within free-tier OpenWeatherMap API quotas while serving multiple silos at the same cooperative facility, weather forecasts are cached by facility coordinates with a 30-minute TTL and graceful fallback to the most recent cached forecast during rural internet dropouts.
Module 5: Interactive Operator Dashboard
Designed around Month-1 stakeholder feedback, the frontend replaces dense statistical tables with color-coded traffic-light risk badges (Green = Low, Amber = Medium, Red = High), intuitive radial gauges for temperature, humidity, and moisture, a 48–72 hour external weather strip, and plain-language corrective action cards
7.2 Implementation Challenges & Engineering Resolutions
Table 7.1: Key Technical Challenges Encountered and Resolved During Development
Challenge Encountered
Root Cause
Engineering Resolution Implemented
Polyglot Data Synchronization
Bridging high-frequency append-only Cassandra rows with relational graph queries in Neo4j introduced latency spikes.
Decoupled ingestion from graph queries via an in-memory TTL cache for static regional Neo4j priors, reducing inference round-trip overhead.
Class Imbalance in Spoilage Scenarios
Real and simulated storage logs contain far more "Safe (Low Risk)" readings than "Critical (High Risk)" events, risking false negatives.
Applied class-weighted loss functions (class_weight='balanced' in Random Forest and weighted categorical cross-entropy in TensorFlow) plus high-recall probability thresholding.
External Weather API Quotas & Connectivity Drops
Polling OpenWeatherMap per silo exhausts free-tier limits and fails during intermittent rural network outages.
Implemented facility-level coordinate caching (30-min TTL) and graceful fallback to last-known forecast values with staleness indicators.
Operator Usability & Technical Literacy Barrier
Raw probability scores and CQL/graph outputs were confusing to non-technical storage operators during early demos.
Redesigned UI around color-coded risk cards and step-by-step operational cues (when to open vs. close vents based on external vs. internal RH).

Chapter 10: Conclusion & Future Scope
10.1 Conclusion
Post-harvest grain spoilage is a deeply consequential yet technically solvable community challenge affecting smallholder farmer incomes and national food security. Through this CS002 Community Service Project, our team engineered and validated the AI-Powered Grain Spoilage Prevention System for Storage Facilities, uniting high-throughput storage telemetry persistence (Apache Cassandra), live 48–72 hour meteorological forecasting (OpenWeatherMap API), historical climate relationship modeling (Neo4j Knowledge Graph), and dual machine learning risk classification (Random Forest & TensorFlow 2.x) inside an intuitive early-warning web dashboard. By shifting rural grain storage management from reactive visual checks to proactive, weather-aware spoilage prevention, the project delivers a practical, zero-licensing-cost technical foundation aligned with UN SDG 2 (Zero Hunger), UN SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), and UN SDG 13 (Climate Action).
10.2 Future Scope
Beyond this one-semester cycle, the platform can be scaled into a production rural deployment through five targeted engineering enhancements:1. Low-Cost Edge IoT Hardware Integration: Deploy battery-powered ESP32 / LoRaWAN sensor lances (DHT22/SHT31 temperature-humidity probes + capacitive grain moisture sensors + NDIR CO2 sensors for early respiration detection) directly inside grain stacks.2. Multilingual SMS & WhatsApp Alerting: Integrate vernacular Hindi and regional-language SMS/WhatsApp notifications so rural godown managers receive High-Risk alerts even without continuous smartphone internet access.3. Closed-Loop Automated Aeration Control: Connect risk-engine outputs to solar-powered relay switches that automatically activate exhaust and aeration fans only during psychrometrically safe external humidity windows.4. Multi-Season Model Retraining & Edge Inference: Export the TensorFlow 2.x classifier to TensorFlow Lite for offline edge inference during rural connectivity outages, with periodic retraining on multi-season ground-truth spoilage logs.5. Cooperative & Mandi-Level Federation: Scale the multi-tenant Cassandra and Neo4j architecture to monitor dozens of village FPO godowns under a unified district-level food security dashboard.
